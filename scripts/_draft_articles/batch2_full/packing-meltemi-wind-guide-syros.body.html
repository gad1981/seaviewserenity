If you holiday in the Cyclades in summer, sooner or later you will meet the meltemi. It is not a storm and it is not a reason to avoid the islands. It is a steady northerly wind, and it affects what you pack, which beach you choose and how you plan boat days. This guide explains what to expect on Syros, what we suggest bringing and what to do on a windy day.

What the meltemi is

The meltemi is the Greek name for the Etesian winds. According to a guide summarising the Hellenic National Meteorological Service (HNMS) and meteo.gr, these are north winds that blow mainly over the Aegean in the warm part of the year, with peak strength from mid-June to early September. In the central Aegean they commonly reach force 6 to 7 on the Beaufort scale, sometimes 8 to 9. They tend to be strongest around midday and in the afternoon and ease in the evening and at night.[2]

Visit Greece makes the same point from a beach perspective. It says the most popular beaches of Syros are in the south, because that side is protected from the meltemi.[1] It also calls Mykonos the "island of the winds", which gives a sense of how normal wind is in the Cyclades.[4] On some days the air is still and on others it blows hard, so you need a plan for both.

What to pack for a windy week

  • A light windproof layer. Even on a hot day, wind on a terrace or a boat deck feels cooler. A thin jacket weighs almost nothing.
  • A hat that stays on. Choose one with a chin strap, or take a cap, and a scarf or buff.
  • Sunscreen anyway. A breeze hides how strong the sun is. Reapply after swimming.
  • Clips or weights for towels. Beach towels, light clothes and paper napkins fly easily. Bring strong clips.
  • Sunglasses or light eyewear. Blown sand is uncomfortable, especially at an open beach.
  • Closed shoes for walks. Ano Syros and the coastal paths are windy, and the stone steps can be slippery.
  • A dry bag or zip bags. For phones and papers on a boat or a spray-prone shore.
  • Something for seasickness, if you are prone to it. Ask your pharmacist if you're unsure.

How the wind affects ferries

Sea conditions matter more than the wind forecast for your town. In Greece, the local port authority, part of the Hellenic Coast Guard, can suspend departures when conditions are rough, and the limits depend on the type of vessel. High-speed craft are generally stopped earlier than large conventional ferries.[3] Even then, a forecast cannot tell you whether a specific sailing will run. Check the operator's notices, your booking confirmation and the official marine forecast for the right sea area.[7]

Ferryhopper notes that, if a trip is cancelled, you can usually change your ticket to another departure of equal value or ask the operator for a refund.[5] Our advice is simple. Don't schedule a boat on the day before a flight home, and keep a spare day when you can. If you plan to see other islands, our article on day trips to Tinos and Mykonos goes into this.

Plan-B beaches on Syros

Beach choice matters more than most visitors expect. These are the broad patterns Visit Greece gives, and you should treat them as a starting point, not a promise for any given day:

  • The south: Agathopes has golden sand and shallow water, Galissas has a large bay with easy access, and Komito has calm water and a windsurfing centre. All three are on the side Visit Greece says is protected from the meltemi.[1]
  • The west: Kini has tavernas right on the water and a shallow, protected sandy cove with a sunset view. Delfini and Varvarousa are smaller coves reached by a short hike along the road or by boat.[1]
  • The north: More rugged and wild, with beaches such as Lia, Marmari and Megas Lakkos. Grammata is mainly reached by boat from Kini.[1] These are the ones to avoid on a hard-blowing day.

As hosts on the west coast we can tell you that the wind is rarely identical from day to day, and a beach that is rough in the morning can be fine in the evening. We suggest you look at the water from the road before you unpack, and keep a southern bay in reserve. Our beach guide to Kini, Delfini and Galissas compares them in more detail.

Beyond the beach on a windy day

Syros is not only a beach island. Ermoupoli has neoclassical buildings, squares and public buildings, and Ano Syros has medieval alleys around the fortified cathedral of San Giorgio, as Visit Greece describes.[6] Museums, theatres, cafés and a long lunch don't care about the wind. Our Ermoupoli and beach day is a good starting plan, and so is cooking at home, which we cover in our grocery and self-catering guide.

A short checklist

  1. Pack a light windproof layer, a hat with a strap, towel clips and sunscreen.
  2. Check the marine forecast and the operator's notices before any boat day.
  3. Keep one sheltered southern beach as plan B.
  4. Plan one town or culture day for the windiest day of the week.
  5. Leave a spare day around any ferry or flight you can't miss.

The meltemi is part of summer in the Cyclades. With a little flexibility, it rarely spoils a holiday, and it does keep the heat bearable.
