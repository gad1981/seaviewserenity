A 7-Day Syros Itinerary from a West-Coast Villa Base

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-14 · ~10–12 min read

A week on Syros works best when you do not try to see everything. The island is compact, the capital is lively all year, and the beaches are different enough from each other that you can build a rhythm: one active morning, one long swim, one good dinner. Here is how we would spend seven days from a west-coast villa, in our case a house about a ten-minute walk from Delfini Beach. Swap days around freely, especially if the wind decides the beach plan for you.

Day 1: arrive and do almost nothing

Whether you come by ferry or by plane, give yourself a soft landing. Ferries from Piraeus run to Syros all year, with the fastest crossings around two hours.[1] Buy supplies for the first breakfast, open the shutters, and walk down to Delfini Beach before dinner. Visit Greece notes that Delfini and nearby Varvarousa are smaller coves reached by a short walk along the road from Kini, which is exactly the kind of low-effort first evening you want.[2]

Day 2: a slow beach day and Kini at sunset

Stay local. Swim at Delfini in the morning, eat lunch at the house, then drive or walk toward Kini in the late afternoon. According to Visit Greece, Kini has tavernas right on the water, a shallow sandy cove and a beautiful sunset.[2] Plan to arrive before the light turns gold. We have written a separate piece on Kini sunset dining if you want to plan the evening in more detail.

Day 3: Ermoupoli, the capital

Go early, before it gets hot. Ermoupoli is the island’s elegant capital, built in the 19th century with neoclassical mansions, squares and public buildings.[3] Walk the waterfront, cross Miaoulis Square, look into the Apollon Theatre (built in 1864 and inspired by Italian opera houses)[4] and spend a few minutes in the market streets. Syros is famous for loukoumi and halvadopita, and the Ermoupoli market is where local shops sell them.[5] Have a long lunch, then head home for an afternoon swim.

Day 4: Galissas or a southern beach

Use this as your flexible beach day. Visit Greece says the most popular beaches are on the southern side, protected from the meltemi, and highlights Galissas for its large bay, fine sand and easy access.[2] If the north wind has made the west coast choppy, this is your plan B. If it has not, it is simply a different style of beach: more organised, more company, more to do with children.

Day 5: Ano Syros and a museum or two

Ano Syros is the medieval Catholic settlement on the hill above Ermoupoli, a labyrinth of alleys, arches and steps around the cathedral of San Giorgio.[3] It was built by the Venetians in the early 13th century, and the climb from the harbour to the main entrance is around a kilometre.[6] Most visitors drive or take a taxi to the top and walk down. The island’s official guide lists a museum in Ano Syros dedicated to the rebetiko musician Markos Vamvakaris, who was born there.[7] For a full route, see our Ano Syros and Ermoupoli walking guide.

Day 6: the wilder north

If you want a change of scenery, go north. Visit Greece describes northern Syros as more rugged and wild, with Grammata beach reached mainly by boat from Kini, and quiet, clear-water beaches such as Lia, Marmari and Megas Lakkos.[2] Some of these are reached by paths, so wear proper shoes, bring water, and check conditions before you go. Villages like San Michali in Apano Meria give you stone houses and big Aegean views.[8]

Day 7: a last swim, a last lunch

Keep the last day light. Swim at Delfini, pack slowly, and leave plenty of time for the ferry or flight. If you are travelling by sea, plan to be in Ermoupoli with a comfortable margin, since the road from the west coast can take longer than you expect when you are watching the clock. Our guide to getting around covers the ferry-port logistics.

How to adapt the plan

  • With children: keep two beach days, shorten Ermoupoli, and skip the northern hike.
  • In shoulder season: swap swim days for walks and museum visits, and keep a flexible day for weather.
  • For a quiet week: repeat the best day. Delfini in the morning and Kini at sunset is hard to improve.

The point of a villa base is that you are not on a schedule. On Syros, a week can have structure and still feel like a rest.
