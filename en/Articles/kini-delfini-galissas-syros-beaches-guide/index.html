Kini, Delfini, and Galissas: A Practical Syros Beaches Guide

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-21 · ~10–12 min read

Beach names drive a large share of Syros accommodation searches: Kini, Delfini, Galissas, and “best beaches Syros”. Syros is not a one-beach island, and where you stay changes your week. We host on the west coast, near Delfini, so we know that part best, but this guide aims to be fair to all three and to tell you which one to pick for which kind of day. Official guidance is the right starting point before ranking blogs, so we draw on Visit Greece’s beach descriptions throughout.[1]

Galissas: the easy all-rounder

Visit Greece describes Galissas as a large bay with fine sand and easy access, with the chapel of Agia Pakou at the far end, and lists accessibility features for the site.[1] It sits on the more sheltered side of the island, which makes it a dependable choice when the meltemi blows.

  • Best for: a full beach day with children, groups with mixed ages, and windy days elsewhere.
  • Expect: more people in July and August, organised facilities, and tavernas close to the sand.
  • Less ideal if: you want a nearly empty cove or silence.

Kini: sunset and taverna energy

Kini combines a protected, shallow sandy cove with waterfront tavernas and a strong sunset reputation. It is also listed with accessibility facilities, which matters for mixed-age groups.[1] The village keeps a fishing-harbour feel, and you can swim in the afternoon and be at a table by the water when the sun goes down.

  • Best for: a sunset swim followed by dinner, and families who like having tavernas close to the sand.
  • Expect: a busier evening scene in peak season. Book a waterfront table for sunset in July and August.
  • Less ideal if: you want long stretches of empty beach in high season.

Delfini and Varvarousa: quieter coves for a “stay local” rhythm

Delfini and Varvarousa are presented by Visit Greece as smaller west-coast coves near Kini, reached by a short walk along the road or by boat.[1] They are the reason many travellers hunting quieter seaside accommodation prefer a house in this pocket: less transfer time, more repeat swimming and fewer logistics. Our house is about a 10-minute walk from Delfini.

  • Best for: early morning swims, repeat visits, and anyone who wants a simple cove without a beach-club scene.
  • Expect: a small cove with limited facilities. Bring an umbrella, water and snacks, and carry rubbish out.
  • Less ideal if: you need sunbeds, a bar and a lifeguard right at the sand, or if the wind is strong and the sea is rough.

Southern options: Agathopes and beyond

Many of Syros’ best-known sandy beaches are on the south side, more sheltered from the meltemi. Agathopes is singled out by Visit Greece for shallow water and family-friendly facilities.[1] These are the backup on windy days, and they are also the place to go if your group wants a more organised beach afternoon. They are a drive from the west coast, so it is worth checking the wind forecast before you set out.

A sample beach week

  1. Day 1: arrive, shop, walk down to Delfini for a first swim.
  2. Day 2: morning at Delfini, sunset dinner at Kini.
  3. Day 3: Ermoupoli in the morning, a late swim after.
  4. Day 4: Galissas for a full beach day.
  5. Day 5: slow day, with a swim before breakfast, a siesta, and a terrace evening.
  6. Day 6: a southern beach such as Agathopes, or wherever the wind is kindest.

Practical beach tips

  • Check the wind forecast each morning. In the Cyclades, wind direction decides which beach is pleasant.
  • Bring shade. Many small coves have little natural shade.
  • Water shoes help at rocky entries.
  • Take cash for small beach cafés, in case card machines are not working.

Syros’ broader destination story still matters: beaches pair best with Ermoupoli culture rather than competing with it.[2][3] The winning trip is usually a cove, a town, and one southern beach day, not seven identical afternoons on the same shore.

Beach etiquette and safety

  • Leave no trace. Small coves rarely have frequent rubbish collection. Take your litter with you.
  • Respect other people’s space. On a small cove, set up a reasonable distance from others and keep music low.
  • Check conditions before swimming. Strong wind can create currents and waves, so with children or weaker swimmers, choose a sheltered beach.
  • Do not assume lifeguards. Many smaller beaches do not have one.
  • Protect yourself from the sun. Between 12 and 4 pm in summer, the sun is strong and shade is limited. Plan your beach time for morning and late afternoon.

Getting between beaches

From our house, Delfini is a walk, Kini is a short drive or a longer walk, and Galissas is a drive across the island. Keep in mind that parking at popular beaches fills up in July and August, so arrive early or in the late afternoon. If you rely on taxis or buses, check times in advance, particularly for the return trip in the evening.
