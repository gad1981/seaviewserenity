How to Get to Syros from Athens: Ferry, Flight, and Booking Tips

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-21 · ~10–12 min read

If you are searching for “how to get to Syros” or “Athens to Syros ferry”, you probably already like the island and just need a clean arrival plan before booking a seaside house. Syros can be reached by sea or by air from Athens. Most visitors take the ferry from Piraeus into Ermoupoli, and that is the option we recommend for most guests, with a flight as a useful alternative if you are short on time and the schedule works.

Option 1: Ferry from Athens (Piraeus)

The main route is Piraeus to Ermoupoli. Ferryhopper’s route data shows year-round sailings, with up to about six daily crossings in high season, and journey times from roughly 2 hours on the fastest services to around 4 hours on conventional ships.[1] Local destination guidance also lists daily ferry service from Piraeus, with conventional crossings of around four hours and high-speed options closer to two and a half hours, plus other Cyclades connections that make Syros a practical hub.[2]

Fast boat or conventional ferry?

  • Conventional ferry: slower but usually more stable in the wind, with open deck space, more room to move around, and generally easier for car transport. A good choice for families and anyone prone to seasickness.
  • High-speed catamaran: roughly half the time, with seat-only cabins and less outside space. On windy days, which can be common in summer, they can be bumpy, and sailings can be delayed or cancelled when the sea is rough.

Whichever you choose, check the departure port and gate on your ticket and arrive early. Piraeus is a large port with several gates; Cyclades boats are usually signed, but the walk from the metro or taxi drop-off can take longer than you expect. Follow the gate printed on the ticket rather than assuming a fixed berth.

Getting to Piraeus

From central Athens the metro runs to Piraeus. From Athens airport, direct buses and metro-and-bus combinations both reach the port. Allow roughly 60 to 90 minutes from airport to port, plus a buffer for your ferry check-in, so avoid booking a same-day sailing that is too close to your landing time.

Option 2: Flight from Athens

Visit Greece lists a domestic flight from Athens International Airport to Syros Airport of about 25 minutes.[3] It is the fastest option on paper. In practice, check three things: how many flights run on your dates, baggage allowance on the small aircraft, and how you will reach your accommodation, because Syros Airport is small and public transport is limited. Flights can also be affected by the same summer winds as the ferries. If you have a hire car booked and a decent flight time, flying can save most of a day. If you have a lot of luggage or a family, the ferry is usually the easier choice.

Ferry or flight: how to choose

  1. Choose the ferry for flexibility, bringing a car, big luggage, and a scenic arrival into the harbour.
  2. Choose the flight when door-to-door time matters more than cost and seats are available on your dates.[3]
  3. Build in buffer. Do not tie a tight ferry or flight to a long-haul arrival into Athens.

Arriving in Ermoupoli and reaching the west coast

The ferry port is right in Ermoupoli, with cafés, shops, taxis and car-hire desks close by. If you are heading to our house near Delfini Beach, it is a drive of roughly 15 to 20 minutes across the island, depending on traffic and your exact route. We send guests directions and arrival instructions before the trip. Many guests pick up a hire car at the port, do a first supermarket stop in Ermoupoli, and then drive out to the house, which means the fridge is stocked before the first sunset.

Taxis are available but limited in peak season, so if you are not hiring a car, arrange a transfer in advance. Public buses cover the island, but timetables are thin in the evenings.

Booking order that protects your holiday

  1. Lock in accommodation first for peak weeks.
  2. Then ferry or flight. In July and August, popular sailings and vehicle space fill early.
  3. Then car hire, ideally before your tickets are fixed.
  4. Keep your tickets and the host’s phone number in an offline place.

Greece’s South Aegean remains the country’s busiest overnight region, so popular seaside houses and car decks compress in midsummer.[4] Ferries, flights and timetables change between seasons, so always check the operator’s current timetable before you pay.

Once you are on Syros

The island is small, and a hire car makes it easy to reach beaches, villages and restaurants on your own schedule. Book a car in advance for July and August, because supply can run short. If you do not want to drive, taxis and buses exist, but they are limited, especially in the evenings, so you may need to plan transfers around the timetable.

Seasickness, luggage and other practical tips

  • Seasickness: choose a conventional ferry if you are sensitive, sit near the middle of the boat, stay on deck in fresh air and take medication before departure, not after.
  • Luggage: larger bags are normally stored in a luggage area on the ferry. Keep your essentials, medicines and valuables with you.
  • Snacks: boats have cafés, but queues can be long in summer. A bottle of water and a snack make the trip easier, particularly with children.
  • Departure day: allow plenty of time to return a hire car, reach the port and find your boat. If you are flying home from Athens, keep a generous buffer between your ferry arrival and your flight.
