Getting Around Syros: Car Rental, Buses, Taxis, and When You Can Skip the Car

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-15 · ~10–12 min read

The question we hear most from guests booking a house on the west coast is simple: do we need a car? The honest answer is that it depends on how you like to travel. Syros is small enough to manage with buses and taxis if you stay in or near Ermoupoli. If you are based by the sea, as we are near Delfini Beach, a car turns a nice week into a flexible one. Here is how the options compare, and how to handle the ferry port on arrival and departure day.

Arriving: the port comes first

Most visitors arrive by ferry. Ferries between Piraeus and Syros run all year, with up to six daily crossings in high season and the fastest trips taking around two hours.[1] Slower conventional ferries take longer; the local lodgings association quotes about four hours by conventional ferry and two and a half by high-speed, and also lists Rafina as a shorter route of around an hour and a half.[2] There are also short flights from Athens to Syros, about 25 minutes.[3]

The ferries dock in Ermoupoli, the capital. From there, your villa on the west coast is a drive or a taxi ride away, so decide before you travel how you will cover that last stretch. The official Syros travel guide recommends checking schedules and fares directly with ferry operators or a travel agent, since timetables change by season.[4] Do the same, and leave margin at both ends of the trip.

Option 1: rent a car

For a villa base outside the capital, this is the most flexible choice. You can reach a sheltered beach when the wind changes, drive to dinner in Kini, make a supermarket run, and see the north without planning around a timetable. The island’s official guide notes that the popular southern beaches are easy to reach by car or bus.[5]

  • Book ahead for peak weeks. Cars on small islands run out in July and August, particularly automatics and larger models.
  • Choose a small car. Village streets are narrow, and in Ermoupoli and Ano Syros parking takes patience. A compact car is easier.
  • Arrange the pickup. Many rental companies can meet you at the port or airport, but confirm this when you book, particularly for early or late ferries.
  • Check parking. The island’s official guide has a parking page covering Ermoupoli, Ano Syros and the villages, which is worth a look before your first town day.[6]

Option 2: use the buses

Syros has a public bus network, run locally by KTEL Syros, and the island’s official timetable pages list the local buses and a mini bus service separately.[7] Buses can work well if you are staying in a village on a main route and plan to go to Ermoupoli and one or two beaches. We would not build a whole villa week around them, for three reasons: frequencies change by season, the last bus back may be earlier than you expect, and a west-coast cove at the end of a bus ride can mean a long wait on the way home.

Our advice is to check the live timetable for the exact dates of your stay, plan one or two trips around it, and treat the bus as a backup rather than the whole plan. We have deliberately not quoted times here, since they change.

Option 3: taxis

Taxis are useful for specific jobs: getting from the port to your house with luggage, going out for dinner when you would rather not drive, or reaching Ano Syros without hunting for a space. They are less practical as a daily transport plan because, on a small island, you may have to book ahead and the fare adds up over a week. If you plan to use taxis for early ferries, arrange the pickup the day before, and ask your host for a contact. We are happy to help guests with this.

Which one for which stay?

  • Short Ermoupoli stay, no beach plans: walk, plus the occasional taxi.
  • A week at a west-coast house: rent a car, at least for most of the week. You can always rent for fewer days and take a taxi for arrival.
  • Couples who like routine: a car for four or five days, buses and taxis for the rest.
  • Families with luggage and kids: a car for the whole stay. Beach bags, towels and snacks are easier that way.

Practical tips for ferry days

  1. Book ferries first, then the car, then restaurant tables for your first evening.
  2. Arrive at the port early on departure day. Allow time for parking, returning the car and boarding.
  3. Check the weather before an important crossing. Strong summer winds can affect Aegean sailings, so keep some flexibility.
  4. Keep your first night simple. A supermarket stop on the way and a swim before dinner beats a complicated restaurant plan.

One last arrivals tip: screenshot the villa pin and the parking note before you lose signal in the port scrum. Syros is easy once you are rolling; the first twenty minutes are when tired travellers make wrong turns. If you land late, consider taxi to the house and car collection the next morning—sleep beats pride.[1][4]
