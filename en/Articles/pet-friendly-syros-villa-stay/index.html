Pet-Friendly Syros Villa Stays: What to Ask Before You Bring the Dog

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-22 · ~10–12 min read

If a dog is part of your family, finding a holiday home that welcomes it can decide the whole trip. Here is the honest version of how it works at a house like ours on Syros. A pet is never automatically included in a booking. Please ask before you book, wait for a clear yes from us, and read the rules below, because several of them are set by Greek law and not by us.

First: ask the host

At Sea View Serenity we treat pets as a case-by-case question. Before you send a booking request, message us with the type of animal, its size, its age and how it behaves with strangers, other dogs and being left alone. We would rather have that conversation early than have a problem on arrival day. Also check the pet setting on whichever booking platform you use, and keep our written reply, so nobody is surprised later.

Questions worth asking any host, not only us:

  • Is the animal allowed in the house at all, and are there rooms or furniture it should stay off?
  • Are there any extra cleaning terms, and what should be done with bedding and towels?
  • How are the terrace, steps and walls laid out? On our terrace, for example, there are stone steps and low walls (see the photo below), so a puppy or an adventurous dog needs watching.
  • What should you do about barking? Neighbours on a quiet coast notice it.

Dogs on Greek beaches: what the rules say

This is where visitors are most often confused. Greece's national rule is set out in article 19 of Law 4830/2021. According to the government's pet-welfare portal, dogs may go onto unorganised beaches and into the sea, but out of the water they must always be on a lead. On organised beaches, dogs are allowed only if they are assistance dogs, or if the operator has put up a clear sign allowing them. On Blue Flag beaches, dogs are not allowed, with the exception of assistance dogs.[1][2]

The same portal reminds owners to keep control of the dog, pick up after it straight away, respect other bathers, provide water and shade, and never leave a dog in a parked car.[1] Municipalities and port authorities can add local rules, and a sign at the entrance is what applies on the day.[6] If a sign says no dogs, that is the answer, whatever any article says. For anything you are unsure of, the local tourism office listed on the official Syros guide is the right place to ask.[4]

We are not going to tell you that a particular beach on Syros is "dog-friendly". That depends on whether it is organised, whether there is signage and what the municipality decides in a given season. What we can say is how the beaches around us look from a practical standpoint.

The beaches around Delfini and Kini

Visit Greece describes the west of the island as quieter: Kini has tavernas right on the water and a shallow, protected sandy cove, while Delfini and Varvarousa are smaller coves reached by a short hike along the road or by boat.[3] In the photo above you can see umbrellas on the sand at Kini, which is the kind of thing to look for. If a stretch has sunbeds and umbrellas run by an operator, play it safe and treat it as organised: look for a sign and ask the operator before going in with a dog. If you are on a small, open cove with no facilities, you are likely on an unorganised beach, but check for signs and follow the lead rule.

Our practical tips for dog owners:

  • Go early or late. Midday summer heat is hard on dogs, and sand can be too hot for paws.
  • Bring your own water and shade. Small coves don't have a shop next door.
  • Carry bags. Pick up after your dog, on the beach and on the road.
  • Plan the walk. A short hike along a road to a small cove means a lead, water and attention to passing cars.

Daily routine that keeps pets safe

Walk early, rest midday, walk again at dusk. Offer water more often than you think necessary. Rinse sandy paws before they sand-blast the stone floors. Bring a familiar blanket so the villa smells less alien on night one.

If your dog barks at scooters or village cats, use a quieter back path and shorter sessions. Neighbours on Syros are generally understanding; repeated disturbance is not. We will be direct if a stay is not a fit for a particular animal.

Emergency vet access is more realistic via Ermoupoli than via a tiny cove. Keep your host contact handy and know where you would drive if something went wrong.[1][3] That seriousness is part of loving travel with pets.

And please: never surprise a listing with an undeclared animal. It burns trust across the island’s small host community.

Getting a dog to Syros by ferry

For most people the ferry is the main hurdle. Blue Star Ferries says passengers travelling with a companion animal must declare it when reserving, so a free ticket can be issued. Tickets cannot be issued on board. The ticket needs the pet's health book or passport number, its microchip number, its category and its size (over or under 10 kg).[5]

The same page lists these conditions:

  • Ships have PET cabins (maximum two pets per cabin) and special pet areas. Book early, because they are limited.
  • Pets are not allowed in other cabins, bars, restaurants or indoor public areas.
  • On the open deck, pets must wear a muzzle, be on a lead and be accompanied by a guardian.
  • On high-speed ships, pets are only carried in a small carrier up to 10 kg.
  • Unaccompanied pets are not accepted.[5]

Other operators have their own rules, so check the one you will actually sail with. For the general route and timing, see our guide to getting to Syros from Athens.

What we would pack for a dog

Bring the pet passport and vaccination papers, a lead, a collar with ID, waste bags, a travel water bowl, the dog's own bedding and food, and a towel for sandy paws. A collapsible shade or a light blanket is useful at the beach. Ask us about the nearest vet before you need one, and keep that number in your phone.

If it isn't the right trip for your pet

Sometimes the honest answer is that a particular stay doesn't suit a particular animal, for instance after a long ferry and a hot week. If that's the case, we would rather say so than see an anxious dog and a stressful holiday. Ask us, and we'll tell you what we think.
