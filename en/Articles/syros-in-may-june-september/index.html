Syros in May, June and September: Why Shoulder Season Suits a Villa Stay

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-18 · ~10–12 min read

If you can choose your dates, the shoulder months on Syros are hard to beat. May, June and September give you long light, a sea that ranges from fresh to properly warm, and an island that is open but not crowded. We host guests at a house near Delfini Beach, and these are the months when we most often say: if you are flexible, come now. Here is what to expect in each, using the island’s own climate figures and a few honest caveats, with planning in mind for next season.

What the numbers say

The island’s official travel guide notes that Syros has a generally mild climate, with roughly 3,000 hours of sunshine a year. Its monthly chart lists average temperatures of about 19°C in May, 23°C in June, 25°C in July and August, 23°C in September and 19°C in October. Rainfall drops to around 18 mm in May, 5 mm in June and 18 mm in September, against 39 mm in October. Daily sunshine is listed at about 12 hours in May, 13 in June and 11 in September.[1] These are averages, and individual years vary, but the pattern is clear: June is the driest of the three, and both ends of the season are pleasantly warm.

May: green, bright and a little cool in the sea

May is for walkers and people who like an island at its freshest. Hills are still green, the days are long and Ermoupoli is lively. Syros keeps its life going all year, so you will find the capital open and its cafés in use.[2] The sea is the main question: it is still cool, and some people will find it too cold for long swims. Plan a mix of walking, town and short dips, and treat any warm, calm day as a bonus. Some seasonal beach businesses are only opening in May, so do not expect every beach bar to be running at full speed.

Easter often falls in April or early May and is a special time on Syros. Syros is one of the few places where Orthodox and Catholic Easter are celebrated on the same day, with processions and services in Ermoupoli, Ano Syros and the villages.[3] It is a wonderful week to be there, but it is also a major holiday week, so book travel and accommodation early.

June: our favourite balance

By June, the days are at their longest, rain is rare and the sea is warming. This is the month when you can have a proper swim in the morning and still find a free table at dinner. It is also when a house with a terrace shines: breakfast outside, long evenings, and no rush. School holidays have not yet begun in many countries, so beach mornings are often quiet. Late June can start to feel more like summer, so if your priority is empty sand, aim for the first half of the month.

September: warm sea, softer crowds

September is the swimmer’s month. The sea has had a summer to warm up, the average air temperature is still around 23°C,[1] and the August crowds have gone home. Evenings are gentler and the light is lower and golden, which suits sunset dinners at the waterfront in Kini. Expect more variable weather late in the month, with the first rainy days arriving. Some businesses start to reduce hours toward the end of the month, so ask ahead if you have a specific plan.

Wind and the west coast

The meltemi is a strong northerly wind that is most typical in summer. Visit Greece notes that the most popular beaches on Syros are in the south, protected from it, while the west coast offers quieter options such as Kini, Delfini and Varvarousa.[4] In the shoulder months the wind is often gentler, but not always, so keep a plan B. From a west-coast house, a short drive to a southern bay such as Galissas gives you one.

Crowds, ferries and booking

Ferries from Piraeus run all year, with more crossings in high season and the quickest taking around two hours.[5] That means shoulder-season travel is straightforward, but sailings are fewer than in July and August, so book the ferry before you finalise the rest of the trip. For the house itself, peak weeks go first. If you want a specific June or September week, the best advice is to ask early; if you are flexible, you will usually have choice.

  • May: book early if your dates include Easter. Pack a light jacket and walking shoes.
  • June: the best all-round choice. Book a few months ahead for the weeks you prefer.
  • September: book earlier for the first half, since it is popular with couples and groups of friends.

Which month for which traveller?

  • Swimmers who feel the cold: late June and September.
  • Walkers, photographers, culture-first travellers: May.
  • Couples wanting long dinners outside: June or early September.
  • Families with school-age children: likely July or August, but a September week outside school calendars is a bonus for families who can choose.

What to pack differently by month

May: warmer evening layer, possible light rain shell. June: classic summer kit plus one shirt for breeze. September: same as June, plus readiness for the first sharp wind shifts.[2]

In all three months, book the house with self-catering in mind. Shoulder restaurants can be wonderful and still run shorter hours than August. A villa kitchen covers the gaps without killing the mood.[1][3]

Thinking about shoulder dates? Look at the villa, then check which May, June or September weeks are open. Check availability
