Kini Restaurants and Sunset Dining on Syros’s West Coast

By your Sea View Serenity hosts · Updated 2026-11-16 · ~10–12 min read

If you ask us where to be at the end of a summer day on Syros, the answer is usually the same: the waterfront at Kini, with a table facing west. Kini is the main village on this stretch of coast, and Delfini, a quieter cove, is nearby. We stay at a house about ten minutes’ walk from Delfini Beach, so we use both in the same way: Delfini for a calm swim, Kini for the evening. We are not going to list restaurant names here, since menus, owners and opening months change. Instead, here is what to expect and how to plan a good evening.

What Kini is like

Visit Greece describes Kini as a place that combines tavernas right on the water with a shallow, sandy, protected cove and a beautiful sunset.[1] That summary is accurate. It is a small fishing village that has become a beach village, with a harbour, a curved bay and a line of waterfront tavernas, plus a few cafés and shops behind them. The island’s official guide lists Kini among the coastal villages known for popular beaches and seaside tavernas,[2] and as one of the island’s Blue Flag beaches with facilities including showers, toilets, changing rooms and access for people with disabilities.[3]

How to approach waterfront tavernas

There is no need to chase a specific name. Most waterfront tavernas in Kini follow a familiar island pattern: simple Greek menus, fish and seafood, grilled meats, salads, local cheeses and house wine, with tables right by the sand or the harbour wall. What makes the difference is timing and choosing a table you like.

  • Walk the waterfront first. Look at the menus, the tables and the view, then choose. It takes five minutes.
  • Ask what is fresh today. In Greek seaside tavernas, the daily catch is often not on the printed menu, and fish is typically priced by weight. It is fine to ask for the price before you order.
  • Order to share. Greek meals work best as several small dishes in the middle of the table, which suits groups of any size.
  • Try island produce. Syros is known for San Michali PDO cheese, kopanisti, local sausages and cured pork louza, and for sweets such as loukoumi and halvadopita.[4][6]

Timing the sunset

Kini faces west, so sunsets are the point. In midsummer, the sun goes down late; in September it goes down earlier and the light is softer. Our rule of thumb: arrive 45 to 60 minutes before sunset, order a drink, and let the table fill with dinner as the sky changes. On a busy August weekend, reserve a table, or go slightly earlier. In May, June or late September you can usually walk in.

Check the wind as well. On strong meltemi days, the west coast can be gusty, and some waterfront tables are more sheltered than others. Visit Greece notes that Syros’ most popular beaches are in the south because they are more protected from the northerly wind, while the west coast is quieter.[1] If the evening is windy, a table with a wall or a sheltered side is a better choice than the front row.

Delfini as your quiet swim base

We like pairing the two. Delfini is smaller and quieter, a short walk along the road from Kini according to Visit Greece,[1] and it works well for an early swim when the water is calm and the sand is empty. Then you can spend the evening in Kini without needing to share the sea with the entire village at six o’clock. If you stay in a house by Delfini, you can walk down in the morning and drive or stroll to Kini for dinner. It is a simple rhythm, and it holds up for a whole week.

Something to do before or after dinner

Kini also has an aquarium, listed among the island’s sights, which can be a good late-afternoon stop with children.[5] If you want to go further, Visit Greece says the famous Grammata beach in the north, known for its white rocks and ancient inscriptions carved into the stone, is reached mainly by boat from Kini.[1] Check boat availability and conditions locally before planning around it.

A relaxed Kini evening, step by step

  1. Swim at Delfini in the afternoon and head back to shower.
  2. Drive or walk to Kini about an hour before sunset.
  3. Stroll the waterfront and choose a table with a view west.
  4. Order drinks and a few dishes to share, and ask about the catch.
  5. Stay for dessert and the afterglow. Island sunsets keep going for a while after the sun drops.

What “west-coast dining” is not

It is not Mykonos club energy and it is not a guaranteed Michelin hunt. It is grilled food, Greek hospitality, and a sky that does most of the theatre.[1] If that is your taste, Kini will feel like the point of the holiday.

Drive carefully on the return in the dark; village roads are shared with scooters and walkers. Better yet, appoint a driver before the first carafe arrives. Tomorrow’s Delfini swim depends on tonight’s sensible choices.[2][3]
